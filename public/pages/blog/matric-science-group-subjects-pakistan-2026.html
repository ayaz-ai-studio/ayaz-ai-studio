Matric Science Group Subjects in Pakistan — Complete 2026 Guide

October 18, 2026 · 9 min read

Picking the Science Group in Class 9 is the first serious academic decision most Pakistani students ever make. It shapes the next four years of study — and quietly closes or opens doors to medicine, engineering, and computer science. Yet most students choose it the way they choose a cricket team: because their friends did, or because their parents said so. This guide lays out exactly what you will study, how it differs from board to board, and how to decide the one choice inside the choice — Biology or Computer Science.

What the Science Group actually is

In Pakistan, "Matric" means Classes 9 and 10, examined by your board at the end of each year. The Science Group is one of the streams you can pick for these two years. It is built around four science and mathematics electives sitting on top of a common compulsory core. The total is usually eight examined subjects across the two years, worth about 1100 marks in the traditional scheme. A revised national scheme now being adopted raises that to 1200 marks and adds Translation of the Holy Quran to the compulsory list, so check your own board's current scheme of studies — boards are in transition and the exact paper pattern for your session matters more than any guide.

The compulsory subjects (same on every board)

Whatever board you are on — Punjab, Sindh, KPK, Balochistan, Federal, or Aga Khan — four subjects are compulsory for everyone in Matric:

  • English (Compulsory) — grammar, composition, and textbook lessons across both years.
  • Urdu (Compulsory) — in Sindh, students may take Asan Urdu in Class 9 and Sindhi in Class 10, with a simpler "Sindhi Salees" option for non-Sindhi speakers.
  • Islamiat / Islamic Education — non-Muslim students take Ethics instead.
  • Pakistan Studies — usually examined in Class 10, covering the history and geography of Pakistan.

These four carry 500 marks combined. They are the same whether you take Science, Computer Science, or Arts — so your stream decision only affects the remaining four subjects.

The four science electives

Here is the heart of the Science Group:

  • Physics — motion, forces, electricity, light, and modern physics, with practicals. The subject students fear most and, oddly, the one that rewards consistent practice the fastest.
  • Chemistry — atomic structure, chemical bonding, acids and bases, organic chemistry basics, with practicals. Heavy on memorization plus numerical problems.
  • Mathematics — algebra, geometry, trigonometry, and matrices. The make-or-break subject: weak math sinks Physics and Chemistry too, because both lean on it.
  • Biology OR Computer Science — you pick one. Biology covers cells, human systems, plants, and ecology with practicals. Computer Science covers programming fundamentals, operating systems, office automation, data communication, and web basics. This single choice splits the Science Group into two futures.

How the boards differ

The national scheme is the skeleton, but each board adds its own flesh:

Punjab (BISEs). Follows the national scheme directly. Textbooks come from the Punjab Curriculum and Textbook Board. One board, Rawalpindi, has piloted new skill-based Matric groups — Health Sciences, Agricultural Sciences, IT, Fashion Designing, and Entrepreneurship — but these are not yet standard across the province, so confirm what your school actually offers.

Sindh. Same science subjects, but Sindhi is a compulsory language subject. In Class 9, Sindhi-speaking students take Sindhi as a 75-mark subject; the common pattern is Asan Urdu in Part I and Sindhi in Part II. The Computer Science syllabus is published by the Sindh Textbook Board and leans practical.

KPK. Same national science scheme, with Pashto taught as a language subject from primary upward. The provincial government has directed that Pashto become compulsory up to grade 12, but implementation has stalled over teacher and textbook shortages — so treat it as a school-level subject for now, not a board exam requirement, unless your school says otherwise.

Balochistan. Follows the national scheme with no major subject divergence found; the Balochistan Textbook Board publishes the books and BISE Quetta conducts the exams.

Federal Board (FBISE). Offers four SSC groupings: Science, Humanities, Technical, and Matric Tech. In the Science Group you take Physics, Chemistry, Mathematics, and Biology or Computer Science or a technical subject. FBISE is also mirroring the revised 1200-mark national scheme, which adds Translation of the Holy Quran and replaces General Mathematics with Functional Mathematics in the Humanities stream.

Aga Khan Board (AKU-EB). A national board with English-medium assessment built around understanding rather than memorization — its papers weight multiple-choice, constructed-response, and extended-response questions toward comprehension and application. Same subject skeleton as the national scheme.

Biology vs Computer Science: the real decision

This is the choice that actually matters, because it quietly pre-selects your Class 11 stream:

  • Pick Biology if you want medicine, dentistry, pharmacy, nursing, or any life-science field. FSc Pre-Medical in Class 11 requires Biology — there is no way around it.
  • Pick Computer Science if you are drawn to software, programming, or IT. It keeps ICS and Pre-Engineering options open and gives you two years of programming head start.

A common myth: "Biology students can switch to CS later, but CS students cannot do medicine." The first half is true — many universities accept Biology-background students into computing with a deficiency course. The second half is also true — without Matric Biology you cannot enter FSc Pre-Medical, and without that, MBBS and BDS are closed. So if medicine is even a maybe, take Biology. If you are sure about computing, take Computer Science and never look back.

Marks, practicals, and what examiners reward

Physics, Chemistry, and Biology (or Computer Science) all include practical examinations — typically 10 marks per subject per year on most boards. Students routinely ignore practicals until the last month; do not. They are the cheapest marks in Matric. Theory papers reward structured answers: definitions first, then explanation, then example. Examiners on Pakistani boards read hundreds of copies — a clean, labeled diagram in Physics or Biology can rescue an otherwise average answer.

Who should actually pick the Science Group?

Pick it if you enjoy figuring out how things work and you are willing to do daily mathematics practice. Do not pick it because "toppers take science" or because your parents dream of a doctor in the family. The Arts and Humanities groups are not a failure track — they lead to law, civil service, media, and business. The worst outcome is two years of Physics you hate, ending in marks too low for the very medical college the choice was supposed to buy.

How to prepare from day one of Class 9

  1. Fix mathematics first. One hour of math practice daily beats five hours on Sunday. Everything in Physics and Chemistry stands on it.
  2. Make your own notes, in your own words. Copying the textbook teaches your hand, not your head. If you cannot explain a concept in one paragraph, you do not know it.
  3. Do the practicals seriously. Ten marks per subject per year, and the viva questions repeat year after year.
  4. Use the board's own past papers. Pakistani board exams are famously repetitive — five years of past papers reveal the entire pattern.

Bottom line

The Matric Science Group is eight subjects: four compulsory (English, Urdu, Islamiat, Pakistan Studies) and four electives (Physics, Chemistry, Mathematics, plus Biology or Computer Science). The boards differ in language subjects and paper style, not in the core. Choose Biology if medicine is on the table; choose Computer Science if computing is the goal; and whichever you pick, let daily math practice — not last-minute panic — carry you through.
