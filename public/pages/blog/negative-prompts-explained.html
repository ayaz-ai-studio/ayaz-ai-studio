Negative Prompts Explained: Telling AI What NOT to Draw

October 8, 2026 · 6 min read

Every AI image tool lets you describe what you want. Fewer beginners discover the second box: the negative prompt, where you describe what you do not want. It is a small feature with an outsized effect, and once you start using it, you will never go back.

What a negative prompt does

During generation, the model is pulled toward your positive prompt and pushed away from your negative prompt. If your images keep coming out with mangled hands, blurry faces, or ugly watermarks, a negative prompt is usually the fastest fix — far faster than regenerating and hoping. Think of it as setting quality guardrails before the model starts.

The universal starter list

Most experienced creators keep a default negative prompt they paste into nearly everything. A solid starting point:

blurry, low quality, distorted, deformed, disfigured, bad anatomy, extra limbs, missing limbs, mutated hands, fused fingers, watermark, text, logo, signature, grainy, oversaturated, dull

Adjust it per use-case: for portraits, emphasize "bad anatomy, deformed face, asymmetric eyes"; for landscapes, "watermark, text" matters more.

When negative prompts backfire

Negation is blunt. "No red" can drain all warmth from an image; "no people" in a street scene can produce an eerily empty world. Also, stuffing the negative prompt with twenty terms can degrade overall quality, because the model spends capacity avoiding things instead of rendering your subject. Rule of thumb: 5–12 well-chosen terms beat a laundry list.

Negative prompts for photorealism

The plasticky, "AI look" often comes from the model defaulting to over-smooth rendering. Counter it with: "cartoon, 3d render, illustration, smooth skin, plastic, airbrushed, oversaturated". Pair that with positive words like "film grain, natural skin texture, candid photo" and the results get dramatically more believable.

Advanced: negative prompting as style control

Beyond fixing defects, negatives sculpt style. Want a minimalist logo? Negate "detailed, photorealistic, shadows". Want a bright children's illustration? Negate "dark, horror, grungy, photorealistic". Each negation narrows the model's options toward your target — it is subtraction as a creative tool.

Putting it into practice

Next time you generate, run the same prompt twice: once with an empty negative prompt, once with the starter list above. Compare them side by side. The difference — cleaner hands, sharper faces, no stray text — will convert you permanently. Save your favorite negative prompt somewhere handy; you will reuse it daily.

Building your personal negative library

One list does not fit all. Build three: a general quality list (blurry, low quality, watermark, text), a people list (bad anatomy, deformed hands, asymmetric eyes, extra fingers), and a style list you adjust per project (cartoon, 3d render for photos; photorealistic, photo for illustrations). Save them in a note app. Over time you will develop a feel for which five terms matter for each kind of image — and your first-try success rate will climb steeply.

Model-specific notes

Some newer models handle negatives more gracefully than older ones, and a few tools hide the negative prompt behind "advanced settings" or call it "things to avoid". A handful of models even accept natural-language avoidance inside the main prompt ("a forest with no people"). If your tool lacks a negative box, try appending avoidance phrases to the main prompt — it is weaker than a dedicated negative prompt but better than nothing.

Real example: fixing a portrait

Prompt: "photorealistic portrait of a smiling chef in a kitchen, warm light". Without negatives, a common failure is waxy skin and odd teeth. Add positive "natural skin texture, subtle smile" and negative "airbrushed skin, plastic, deformed teeth, asymmetric eyes, blurry". The second version typically looks like a different photographer shot it — same idea, professional execution. That gap is the entire value of the negative prompt.

Negative prompts for video

The same principle applies to AI video, where the stakes are higher — a bad frame can ruin a whole clip. Useful video negatives: "morphing, flickering, warping, inconsistent, jittery, sudden changes". These target temporal artifacts directly. Combined with positive "smooth motion, consistent character", they meaningfully improve output stability. Video negatives will not fix a fundamentally broken generation, but they raise the floor of your average result.

When to skip negatives entirely

There are cases where negatives add little: highly stylized work (anime, flat illustration) where "defects" are part of the aesthetic, and very short prompts where the model needs all its capacity for the subject. If your generations look good without them, do not add negatives out of habit — every constraint has a cost, and unnecessary ones narrow your results for no benefit.

Testing your negative prompt

Do not set and forget. Run a simple A/B test: generate the same prompt three times with your negative list and three times without it, then compare the batches honestly. Sometimes you will find a term that was helping with hands but dulling colors — split it into a context-specific list instead of the default. Your negative prompt should evolve as your subjects change; review it monthly and prune terms that no longer earn their place.
